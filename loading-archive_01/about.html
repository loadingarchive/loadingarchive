About Loading Archive

Loading Archive is an independent video game release calendar. We track every upcoming game across PC, PlayStation, Xbox and Nintendo Switch — with verified dates, prices, trailers and honest labels for what's genuinely new versus what's a port or remake.

Why this site exists

Most release calendars have one of two problems: they either list only the big blockbusters, or they dump thousands of unlabeled entries where a shovelware title sits next to a major launch and a re-released ten-year-old game looks like a brand-new one. We built Loading Archive to fix that second problem in particular.

Every game in our database is labeled for what it actually is. A Port is the same game arriving on a new platform. A Remake has been visually or technically rebuilt. An Anticipated tag marks the releases that genuinely matter each month. These distinctions are made by a human, not an algorithm — because no data feed on the internet reliably tells you whether the "new" game on a Switch 2 listing is actually new.

Where our data comes from

We combine several sources and cross-check them against each other:

  • Release dates and platforms are aggregated from the RAWG games database and Wikipedia's release lists, then re-verified daily. When sources disagree, we check the publisher's own announcement.
  • Prices come directly from Steam's storefront in USD, EUR and GBP, and are refreshed daily — including live discount tracking, so the price you see is the price right now, not the launch price from months ago.
  • Trailers, screenshots and system requirements come from the official store listings.
  • Trending data is refreshed every hour from live Steam player statistics.

The pipeline runs automatically every night, but the editorial layer on top of it — port/remake classification, anticipated picks, corrections, removal of duplicate and junk listings — is manual and ongoing.

Our labeling policy

Because "re-release" is doing a lot of work in the games industry right now (especially with the Switch 2 generation), we apply a strict two-tier rule:

  • Port — the same game with the same assets on a new platform. Example: Palworld arriving on PS5, or Elden Ring's Switch 2 edition.
  • Remake — the game has been meaningfully rebuilt: new engine, redone visuals, or a ground-up reconstruction. Example: Fatal Frame II: Crimson Butterfly Remake or Rayman Legends Retold.

Both labels always show the original release date, so you can see at a glance that a game is fifteen years old before you get excited about the "release."

Corrections

Release dates shift constantly and no calendar is ever perfect. If you spot a wrong date, a missing game, or a title we've misclassified, tell us — corrections are usually live within a day.

Who runs this

Loading Archive is built and maintained by a solo developer in the Netherlands as an independent project. It is not affiliated with any publisher, platform holder or storefront. You can reach us any time via the contact page.
